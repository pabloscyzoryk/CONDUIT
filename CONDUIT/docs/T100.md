# T-100

T-100 is an experimental autonomous policy in the shared Rust engine. It creates its own entries, rather than converting a Telegram message into an order. Synergy supplies optional direction and price geometry. The existing GOD-X7 configuration remains the default.

AUTO and AUTO-EA are mutually exclusive modes. A T-100 preset requires AUTO-EA; attempting to start it in AUTO is rejected. Its settings cannot be changed while exposure or unresolved execution exists. An accepted configuration change on a confirmed flat account preserves observations, learning and daily risk anchors.

The research CLI uses `--auto-ea --source-name Synergy` explicitly. The preset's format label alone is not the received message's source identity. The legacy default source label remains unchanged for existing replay commands.

## Decision model

The policy contains four independently selectable entry models: trend pullback, range breakout, range reversion and rejection near a Synergy entry zone. It observes completed BID M1 bars and aggregates complete M5/M15 bars. Backtests construct them from the complete tick tape. Live requires completed broker candles instead of estimating extrema from sampled quotes. An empty live candle feed never falls back to sampled-quote OHLC. A missing interval never becomes an invented candle. A long data gap resets market warmup while retaining source context and confirmed trade state.

Trend, efficiency, range position, volatility, transaction spread and source proximity determine whether a setup is eligible. These are explicit heuristic rules, not a claimed trained probability model. Source influence is bounded. Setting its weight to zero permits a controlled market-only comparison. The source-zone model is inactive without source influence.

The entry includes an initial broker SL and TP. The strategy can use a source SL/TP only within explicit distance and direction bounds. It sizes risk from equity excluding broker credit, floors volume to the broker grid, respects the user lot cap and accounts for the remaining portfolio risk and free margin. It does not increase volume to the broker minimum when the allowed risk cannot fund that minimum.

Revision 3 keeps the approved policy/portfolio risk allowance separately from the risk used by the floored volume. After broker price normalization, the adapter uses that original allowance to check the normalized stop, and may reduce volume further. It never increases the volume approved by the policy, its margin limit or its lot caps. This fixes false minimum-lot rejection when SL rounding moves a stop slightly farther away but sufficient approved risk remains. A genuinely insufficient allowance still rejects the entry. Learning continues to use risk from the confirmed entry, stop and filled volume, rather than the unused allowance; actual fills may differ from requested prices.

Risk management includes correlated-position limits, a cooldown after confirmed entry, a daily loss budget, an optional floor under accumulated daily profit, a maximum holding period, an adverse-trend exit, break-even protection and volatility-dependent trailing. Stops only tighten. A small, slowly changing adjustment to each entry model is learned from completed, verified net trade results, with a neutral prior. Partial receipts do not become independent completed-trade labels.

## Source availability

Only messages attributed by routing to Synergy enter the context book. The first observed version may be an edit. Its content becomes available when received, without being backdated to the original publication timestamp. A late original does not replace an already observed edit.

Pending source setups remain in memory indefinitely until an explicit reply cancellation, including cancellations linked through a reply chain. Context influence can decay or become immaterial at distant prices without deleting the setup. A publisher cancellation removes context eligibility; it does not act as an unconditional command to close autonomous positions.

Revision 2 additionally evaluates the remaining source geometry at the current price. A setup beyond its source SL or final target supplies no directional encouragement. Influence decreases near the final target. This does not cancel a pending setup: its context can become relevant again if price later returns into the source geometry. These are hypotheses about useful information, evaluated separately from source-message validity.

Final Telegram exports cannot prove which earlier text versions were available in live trading. Results using reconstructed or final text must be labelled separately from results using recorded deliveries.

## Execution and state

`core/src/t100` owns observations, policy and learned state. `core/src/engine/t100_execution.rs` owns conversion of intentions into broker operations. Both the live adapter and the simulator use this shared engine. Legacy entry copying and position-management actions are bypassed for an enabled T-100 policy. Common broker reconciliation and account protections remain applicable.

An intention is not a confirmed trade. Confirmed executions create real engine baskets. Uncertain submissions retain their identity and block additional exposure until the broker confirms the exact submission. Recovery never resends an uncertain opening order merely because its initial acknowledgement was missing.

The checkpoint binds configuration, policy revision, market buffers, context, learning, execution reconciliation and basket numbering. Floating-point values use the forensic exact encoding. Missing, incompatible or invalid state requires review before new exposure. This is not a claim of durable write-ahead transaction acknowledgement or exactly-once execution across every possible operating-system crash.

The current checkpoint revision is `T-100/3`. Older T-100 checkpoints do not certify the new approved-budget field and are rejected for review rather than silently migrated. Revision 2 research snapshots and results remain separate; they must not be relabelled as results of the corrected sizing adapter. Disabled T-100 leaves the GOD-X7 execution path unchanged.

Broker timestamps and UTC sessions are distinct. The policy subtracts the explicit broker UTC offset for session eligibility. Daily risk uses broker trading-day boundaries. Historical offset changes and incomplete input streams require their own data verification.

## Evaluation requirements

Optimization uses a maximum lot size of 5. Candidate selection must compare profit, equity drawdown, real daily drawdown, activity and concentration, with all tested configurations retained. Real daily drawdown is `max(0, starting daily equity - minimum daily equity)`, divided by starting daily equity for its percentage.

Full-window compounding from USD 600 is reported alongside GOD-X7 on matched data and costs. Fixed-lot 0.01 runs are the appropriate place for removing the best one or three days when measuring concentration. Compounding outcomes must not be reconstructed by subtracting historical cash profits while leaving the subsequent position sizes unchanged.

Cold-start daily, weekly and monthly tests use separate run windows. In-run engine replacement or account resets are rejected for T-100 until their full autonomous state lineage is qualified. A previously examined period is not described as an untouched holdout. A partial trading day is not a full-day validation sample.

Shared policy code is necessary for decision parity but insufficient to prove it. The delivered market observations, bar construction, source delivery order, broker replies and initial state must also match. Simulator profits do not certify actual broker fills or future profitability. T-100 is not promoted to the default based solely on a backtest or a target of 90–100% positive days.

## Research basis

[Time series momentum](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/mosk.pdf) motivates a trend hypothesis, but its multi-month futures evidence does not establish an intraday gold edge. [Volatility-Managed Portfolios](https://amoreira2.github.io/alan-moreira.github.io/VolPortfolios_published.pdf) and [On the performance of volatility-managed portfolios](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf) provide differing empirical results; ATR sizing here is a separate hypothesis, not a replication of either paper.

[The probability of backtest overfitting](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf) and [The Deflated Sharpe Ratio](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf) motivate retaining losing trials, controlling selection and using chronological evaluation. Neither supplies a certificate of out-of-sample performance for this strategy.

The MetaQuotes documentation for [OnTick](https://www.mql5.com/en/docs/event_handlers/ontick), [OnTradeTransaction](https://www.mql5.com/en/docs/event_handlers/ontradetransaction) and [strategy testing](https://www.mql5.com/en/docs/runtime/testing) describes event and simulator behavior that must be tested separately from policy arithmetic.
